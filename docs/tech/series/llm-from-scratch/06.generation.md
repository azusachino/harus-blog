## 1. The Autoregressive Generation Loop

Autoregressive text generation operates on a simple recursive principle: **yesterday's predictions become today's context.**

Given a user-provided prompt sequence:

$$x_{1:t} = [x_1, x_2, \dots, x_t]$$

The model produces new text by repeating a single-step inference cycle:

```text
1. Pass the current sequence x_{1:t} into the model via a forward pass;
2. Extract the unnormalized logits at the final position: z_t in R^V;
3. Apply a sampling strategy to select the next token ID: x_{t+1};
4. Append the newly selected token to the context: x_{1:t+1} = [x_{1:t}, x_{t+1}];
5. Check stopping criteria (e.g., token matches <|endoftext|> or length reaches max_new_tokens);
6. If not finished, increment t = t + 1 and repeat from step 1.
```

### The Computational Divergence: Training vs. Inference

- **Training**: Thanks to the causal attention mask, an input sequence of length $T$ is processed **in a single forward pass**. The model computes loss across all $T$ positions simultaneously in $\mathcal{O}(1)$ network executions.
- **Inference**: Because future tokens do not yet exist, the network **cannot predict steps ahead in parallel**. Producing $N$ output tokens strictly requires $N$ sequential forward passes. This dependency profile makes large model inference heavily **memory-bandwidth bound**.

## 2. Why Greedy Search Fails

After obtaining the final logit vector $z_t \in \mathbb{R}^V$, the most direct selection rule is **Greedy Search (Argmax)**: always choose the token with the highest predicted probability:

$$x_{t+1} = \arg\max_{k \in \{1, \dots, V\}} z_t[k]$$

While greedy decoding is well-suited for deterministic tasks like code generation or structured JSON extraction, it introduces two major issues in open-ended prose:

1. **Degenerate Repetition Loops**:
   Greedy search frequently traps the generator inside closed cyclic loops. For example, once the model outputs `"in other words"`, the highest-probability continuation often loops back into `"in other words"`, creating repetitive output:
   `"The system is designed to provide safety. In other words, it is designed to provide safety. In other words, it is..."`
2. **Loss of Stylistic Nuance**:
   Natural human language thrives on controlled entropy. Corpus analyses show that human authors rarely choose the globally most frequent word at every position; humans balance predictability with creative and diverse vocabulary. Greedy decoding eliminates this stylistic variation.

## 3. Probability Shaping: Temperature Scaling

To sample probabilistically, we convert unnormalized logits into valid probabilities using Softmax.

By introducing a positive hyperparameter—**Temperature ($T > 0$)**—we modulate the sharpness of the resulting distribution:

$$P_i = \frac{\exp(z_i / T)}{\sum_{j=1}^V \exp(z_j / T)}$$

Varying $T$ adjusts the entropy of token selection:

### 1. Low Temperature ($0 < T < 1.0$, e.g., $T = 0.2$)

- Dividing by a small fraction multiplies logit differences (e.g., a gap between 10 and 5 scales to 50 versus 25).
- The Softmax distribution becomes **sharp and peaked**, concentrating nearly all probability mass onto the top candidates.
- **Characteristics**: Focused, conservative, factual, with minimal risk of hallucination or syntactic errors. As $T \to 0$, behavior approaches greedy argmax selection.

### 2. Standard Temperature ($T = 1.0$)

- Preserves the empirical probability distribution learned during training.

### 3. High Temperature ($T > 1.0$, e.g., $T = 1.4$)

- Dividing by values greater than 1 compresses logit variances, pulling candidates closer to the mean.
- The probability distribution **flattens**, granting lower-ranked words a higher chance of selection.
- **Characteristics**: Diverse and imaginative phrasing; however, excessively high temperatures ($T \ge 2.0$) cause grammatical breakdown into nonsensical token soup.

## 4. Truncation Strategies: Top-$k$ and Top-$p$ (Nucleus Sampling)

Even with temperature scaling, sampling across an entire vocabulary ($V = 50,000$ to $150,000$) introduces the **long-tail catastrophe**:

In any given context, the vast majority of tokens are irrelevant. However, Softmax's exponential nature still assigns tiny probabilities (e.g., $10^{-6}$) to thousands of implausible tokens. Over long generated passages, these tail tokens will occasionally be selected, producing jarring inconsistencies.

Two standard truncation methods eliminate this tail:

### Approach A: Top-$k$ Sampling (Fan et al., 2018)

- **Rule**: Sort all tokens by probability and **keep only the top $k$ candidates** (e.g., $k = 40$).
- Mask the logits of all remaining $V - k$ tokens to $-\infty$.
- Re-normalize probabilities across the surviving $k$ candidates and sample from this restricted pool.
- **Limitation**: $k$ is a static cutoff. When next-token context is constrained (e.g., `"The capital of France is ___"`), keeping 40 tokens risks introducing irrelevant choices. Conversely, during open brainstorming where hundreds of continuations are valid, $k = 40$ prematurely constrains valid options.

### Approach B: Top-$p$ Sampling (Nucleus Sampling, Holtzman et al., 2019)

- **Rule**: Rather than fixing the count of tokens, **set a cumulative probability threshold $p \in (0, 1]$** (typically $p = 0.9$ or $0.95$).
- Sort candidates by descending probability and accumulate them until their combined sum equals or exceeds $p$:
  $$\sum_{i=1}^{k^*} P_{(i)} \ge p$$
- Discard all tokens outside this dynamic "nucleus" and re-normalize within the set.
- **Advantage**: The size of the candidate pool **adapts dynamically to context uncertainty**. When confidence is high, the nucleus narrows to 1–2 candidates; when the prompt is open-ended, it expands to dozens of options.

## 5. Repetition Penalties

To deter repetitive phrases, Keskar et al. (CTRL, 2019) proposed applying a **repetition penalty** to logits prior to Softmax.

For any token index $k$ that has already appeared in the preceding context window:

$$z_k' = \begin{cases} z_k / \theta, & z_k > 0 \\ z_k \cdot \theta, & z_k \le 0 \end{cases} \quad (\text{where penalty } \theta > 1.0)$$

By reducing the logit values of previously generated words (typically with $\theta \in [1.1, 1.2]$), the model is nudged to explore alternate vocabulary and avoid repeating phrasing.

## 6. The Production Inference Engine: KV Cache

When running autoregressive generation naively on long sequences, latency degrades rapidly as the context expands.

### The Quadratic Bottleneck of Naive Inference

Recall from Chapters 03 and 04 that each attention block computes Key and Value projections for all input tokens:

- Step 1 (length $T$): Forward pass across $x_{1:T}$ requires $\mathcal{O}(T^2)$ attention operations.
- Step 2 (length $T+1$): The input becomes $x_{1:T+1}$. **The model recomputes Keys and Values for the preceding $T$ tokens from scratch!**
- Step $N$: Total attention computation grows cubically with output length: $\sum_{t=1}^N \mathcal{O}((T+t)^2)$.

### The Principle of KV Caching

Under a causal attention mask, **the Key and Value vectors for token $i$ depend strictly on tokens $1 \dots i$**, unaffected by future tokens $i+1, i+2, \dots$.

Once computed, the Key and Value vectors for historical tokens remain constant for all subsequent generation steps.

**Engineering Design of the KV Cache**:

1. **State Retention**: Each decoder layer maintains pre-allocated cache tensors in GPU memory to store accumulated Key and Value vectors across previous steps.
2. **Single-Token Incremental Steps**: At step $t+1$, the model passes **only the single newly generated token ($x_{\text{new}}$)** through the forward pass.
3. Compute Query, Key, and Value vectors solely for $x_{\text{new}}$: $q_{\text{new}}, k_{\text{new}}, v_{\text{new}} \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times D}$.
4. Concatenate $k_{\text{new}}$ and $v_{\text{new}}$ into the persistent layer caches.
5. Compute dot-product attention between the single query $q_{\text{new}}$ and the full cached Key tensor, aggregating across all cached Values.
6. This reduces per-step compute complexity from $\mathcal{O}(T^2)$ to **$\mathcal{O}(T)$**, transforming sequential generation into a responsive, low-latency streaming pipeline.

## 7. Implementation & Sampling in `calc`

In our companion repository [`calc`](https://github.com/azusachino/calc), the autoregressive generator is implemented inside `LanguageModel` in [`calc/core/model.py`](https://github.com/azusachino/calc/blob/main/calc/core/model.py):

```python
@torch.no_grad()
def generate(self, tokens: torch.Tensor, max_new_tokens: int) -> torch.Tensor:
    for _ in range(max_new_tokens):
        # 1. Truncate context to block_size if sequence exceeds window capacity
        context = tokens[:, -self.block_size :]

        # 2. Forward pass to obtain logits
        logits, _ = self(context)

        # 3. Extract unnormalized logits from the final temporal position: [B, V]
        last_logits = logits[:, -1]

        # 4. Softmax normalization followed by multinomial sampling
        probs = last_logits.softmax(dim=-1)
        next_token = torch.multinomial(probs, num_samples=1)

        # 5. Append newly sampled token to running context
        tokens = torch.cat((tokens, next_token), dim=1)

    return tokens
```

In [`calc/core/sample.py`](https://github.com/azusachino/calc/blob/main/calc/core/sample.py), we load the trained checkpoint `artifacts/model.pt` and initiate generation using a newline character (`\n`) as our prompt:

```bash
make sample
```

### Inspecting Generated Output

Trained on a compact Shakespeare text for 1,000 steps (~17 seconds on CPU), our minimal model produces recognizable dialogue formatting and vocabulary:

```text
First Citizen:
We are accounted poor citizens, the patricians good.
What authority surfeits on would relieve us: if they
would yield us but the superfluity, while it were
wholesome, we might guess they relieved us humanely;
but they think we are too dear: the leanness that
afflicts us, the object of our misery, is as an
inventory to particularise their abundance; our
sufferance is a gain to them.
```

Key patterns captured by the model:

- Script conventions such as character tags followed by colons (`First Citizen:`).
- Accurate English morphology including verb suffixes and participle agreements (`accounted`, `afflicts`, `relieved`).
- Natural line breaks and metric phrasing.

Although implemented in concise PyTorch without heavy external frameworks, this educational model shares its mathematical core with production language models.

### Beyond Base Pre-Training

Completing pre-training yields a foundational base model—a capable statistical continuator. If asked `"What is the capital of France?"`, a base model might simply continue with `"What is the capital of Germany? What is the capital of Italy?"`.

To convert this raw predictor into an attentive, reliable assistant, modern LLM development continues through post-training:

- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**: Transitioning from open-ended text completion to instruction-following and tool-use conversations.
- **Preference Alignment (RLHF / DPO)**: Steering outputs toward helpful, harmless, and honest completions via reinforcement learning and direct preference optimization.
- **Test-Time Compute & Reasoning Scaling**: Advanced architectures (e.g., DeepSeek-R1, OpenAI o-series) that allocate dynamic compute to reinforcement-learning-guided Chain-of-Thought search during inference.

Across every evolution of high-level frameworks, the tensor operations, self-attention interactions, and self-supervised dynamics covered in this series remain the foundation of generative AI.
