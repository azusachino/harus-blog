The transition from AWS Certified Solutions Architect - Professional SAP-C02 to **SAP-C03** marks a fundamental pivot in enterprise cloud architecture. For years, the SAP exam centered on complex multi-account landing zones, Transit Gateway hub-and-spoke topologies, and classic lift-and-shift migration waves. In SAP-C03—generally available as of **November 17, 2026** following SAP-C02's retirement on November 16—the cloud architect's arena has expanded dramatically into **generative AI systems, zero-trust service networking, dynamic workload-aware compute, and automated resilience engineering**.

This masterclass series is built for engineers and architects renewing their credentials or stepping up to the professional benchmark. Rather than reciting console walk-throughs or regurgitating documentation, this series addresses the core challenge of the SAP exam: **multi-constraint architectural trade-offs**. When a scenario demands maximum availability while imposing aggressive RTO/RPO limits under strict cost caps, which service topology genuinely satisfies the contract?

---

## Before you start

This series is for programmers who have used a little AWS, or none, and want to understand how the Solutions Architect Professional exam thinks. The chapters build on each other, so read them in order.

AWS certifications come in levels. The Professional exam gives you long business scenarios, such as a company with 40 accounts, a deadline and a budget, and asks which design satisfies every constraint at once. A chapter on the exam's structure matters because the exam changes: AWS periodically replaces a version (here C02 with C03), drops some services from the syllabus and adds others. Knowing the shape of the exam tells you what to study and what to skip.

Nothing needs to come first. A rough idea of what a virtual server, a database and a network are is enough; the terms below cover the rest.

Terms used in this chapter:

- **SAP-C03**: the current version code of the AWS Certified Solutions Architect - Professional exam.
- **Domain**: one of the weighted topic areas the exam is divided into; the weight is the share of questions drawn from it.
- **Multi-account landing zone**: a pre-built set of separate AWS accounts with shared security and networking rules, used instead of putting everything in one account.
- **Amazon Bedrock**: the AWS service for calling hosted generative AI models through an API.
- **VPC Lattice**: an AWS service that connects and secures calls between applications without you wiring up the network by hand.
- **RPO / RTO**: how much data you can afford to lose after a failure, and how long you can afford to be down.
- **Service mesh**: infrastructure that handles authentication, routing and retries for calls between services.

## 1. Exam Blueprint & Structural Evolution

The SAP-C03 exam retains the standard professional format—**75 questions (65 scored, 10 unscored), 180 minutes, \$300 USD**—but lowers the passing scaled score from 750 to **720** (on a 100–1000 scale) and reorganizes the syllabus from four legacy domains into **five Well-Architected domains**:

| Legacy SAP-C02 Domain | Weight | Revamped SAP-C03 Domain | Weight | Core Focus Shift |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **Domain 1**: Design for Organizational Complexity | 26% | **Domain 1**: Architecture Design and Implementation Built for the Cloud | **25%** | Cloud-native microservices, Bedrock GenAI integration, VPC Lattice service mesh, and event choreographies. |
| **Domain 2**: Design for New Solutions | 29% | **Domain 2**: Security, Compliance, and Governance | **25%** | Resource Control Policies (RCPs), IAM Identity Center ABAC, multi-region KMS, and AI safety guardrails. |
| **Domain 3**: Continuous Improvement for Existing Solutions | 25% | **Domain 3**: Cost-Optimized Architecture Design | **15%** | FinOps governance, Compute Optimizer, S3 Express One Zone tiering, and cross-AZ transfer reduction. |
| **Domain 4**: Accelerate Workload Migration and Modernization | 20% | **Domain 4**: Resilience, Migration, and Business Continuity | **15%** | Route 53 ARC automated routing, Resilience Hub evaluation, FIS chaos testing, and MGN/DMS migration factories. |
| *(no fifth C02 domain)* | — | **Domain 5**: Operational Excellence and Automation | **20%** | Cross-account CloudWatch observability, OpenTelemetry (ADOT), Systems Manager automation runbooks, and self-healing pipelines. |

The rows pair the domains by number, not by lineage. C02 was organised by situation (complexity, new, existing, migration) and C03 by Well-Architected pillar, so most C02 skills survive merged into new tasks: C03 Domain 1 draws mainly on C02 Domain 2 and the networking half of Domain 1, Domain 2 on C02 Domain 1 and the security half of Domain 3, and Domain 5 on the deployment and monitoring parts of C02 Domains 2 and 3.

## 2. In-Scope Additions vs Dropped Services

Knowing what AWS removed from SAP-C03 matters as much as knowing what was added, because it tells you which legacy services you can stop memorizing.

### Services Elevated to Core Exam In-Scope

1. **Generative AI & Agentic Workloads**:
   - **Amazon Bedrock**: Serverless foundation models, Provisioned Throughput vs On-Demand, and PrivateLink VPC endpoints.
   - **RAG Architecture**: Bedrock Knowledge Bases paired with OpenSearch Serverless Vector Engine and Amazon Aurora PostgreSQL `pgvector`.
   - **Responsible AI**: Bedrock Guardrails for PII redaction and prompt safety, plus Bedrock AgentCore tool execution boundaries.
2. **Modern Service Networking & Global Mesh**:
   - **Amazon VPC Lattice**: Cross-VPC and cross-account application networking, client-side DNS routing without overlapping CIDR issues, and IAM-authenticated service policies.
   - **AWS Cloud WAN**: Centralized core network policies (CNP) managing multi-region SD-WAN attachments, replacing complex full-mesh Transit Gateway peering topologies.
3. **Advanced Container Provisioning & Scaling**:
   - **Karpenter**: Just-in-time (JIT) Kubernetes node autoscaling on Amazon EKS, dynamic bin-packing, Spot/Graviton diversification, and rapid node consolidation.
   - **ECS Service Connect**: Envoy-based service discovery and bi-directional traffic management without external load balancers.
4. **Policy Enforcement & Cryptography**:
   - **Resource Control Policies (RCPs)**: Newly introduced preventive guardrails restricting access to resources within an organization from external principals.
   - **Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC)**: Quantum-resistant algorithms on AWS endpoints and in AWS KMS (ML-DSA signing keys, hybrid ML-KEM key exchange for TLS).

### Dropped From the In-Scope Services List

AWS's [SAP-C02 to SAP-C03 comparison](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/aws-certification/latest/solutions-architect-professional-03/sap-03-comparison.html) drops these from the in-scope services list:

- **Deployment & Application Platforms**: AWS Service Catalog, AWS Proton, AWS App Runner, AWS Batch, AWS Amplify, Amazon Lightsail.
- **End-User Computing & Media**: Amazon WorkSpaces, Amazon AppStream 2.0, AWS Elemental media services, Amazon SES.
- **IoT & Edge**: All AWS IoT services, AWS Wavelength.

The same page warns that a concept missing from the blueprint "does not mean that these concepts are fully out of scope". Treat these as low priority, not as answers that are always wrong.

## 3. The Multi-Constraint Scenario Decision Matrix

The signature hallmark of the Professional-level exam is the **multi-constraint scenario**. A typical question presents a 250-word enterprise situation with multiple competing requirements:

> *"A global enterprise requires an architecture that minimizes operational overhead, achieves an RTO under 5 minutes with zero data loss (RPO = 0), prevents cross-account lateral movement, and remains the most cost-effective option."*

When dissecting such questions, always execute a **constraint hierarchy elimination**:

```text
Step 1: Identify the Hard Blockers (Must-Have Constraints)
        └── Example: "RPO = 0" eliminates any asynchronous snapshot or periodic backup mechanism.
Step 2: Identify the Determining Tiebreaker
        └── Example: "Minimizes operational overhead" eliminates custom EC2-hosted replication agents in favor of managed Global Databases.
Step 3: Check Governance & Boundary Alignment
        └── Example: Does the solution enforce IAM identity propagation, or does it violate data perimeter invariants?
Step 4: Verify Cost Efficiency
        └── Example: Between two technically valid architectures, select the serverless or consumption-based model over pre-provisioned idle clusters.
```

## 5. Course Strategy & Study Methodology

To prepare effectively for the SAP-C03 exam:

1. **Think in Declarative Boundaries**:
   Do not think of security as firewall IP rules. Think of security in terms of **Principals, Actions, Resources, and Conditions (PARC)** enforced at the OU level via SCPs and RCPs.
2. **Master the Network Transit Hierarchy**:
   Know exactly when to reach for **VPC Peering** (low latency, same region, non-overlapping), **Transit Gateway** (complex on-prem routing, multicast, inspection VPCs), **AWS Cloud WAN** (global multi-region corporate SD-WAN), and **Amazon VPC Lattice** (microservice-level application connectivity without network peering).
3. **Internalize Data Consistency & Replication Lag**:
   Know the exact RTO and RPO limits of Amazon Aurora Global Database (storage-level physical replication, typically under 1 second lag) vs Amazon DynamoDB Global Tables (multi-active; in the default mode eventually consistent across Regions with last-writer-wins, plus an optional multi-Region strong consistency mode) vs Amazon S3 Cross-Region Replication (RTC guarantees 99.99% replicated within 15 minutes).

## 6. Quantitative SLA, RPO/RTO & Governance Reference Matrix

Before diving into individual domain chapters, commit these quantitative architectural boundaries to memory—they serve as the primary elimination filters across SAP-C03 scenario questions:

| Architecture / Service Pattern | Typical RPO | Typical RTO | Hard Technical Boundary / Quota |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Amazon RDS Multi-AZ (Single Region)** | $0\text{ s}$ (Synchronous physical block replication) | $60\text{--}120\text{ s}$ (Automated DNS flip) | Standby cannot serve read traffic (unless Multi-AZ DB Cluster with 2 readable standbys) |
| **Amazon Aurora Global Database** | $< 1\text{ s}$ (Unplanned) / $0\text{ s}$ (Planned switchover) | $< 60\text{ s}$ | Up to $1$ primary + $5$ secondary regions; up to $16$ read replicas per region |
| **Amazon DynamoDB Global Tables** | $1\text{--}2\text{ s}$ (Asynchronous DynamoDB Streams) | $\approx 0\text{ s}$ (Multi-active local endpoints) | Default mode: last-writer-wins (LWW) conflict resolution and eventually consistent cross-Region reads; multi-Region strong consistency (RPO 0) is available as an opt-in mode |
| **Amazon S3 Replication Time Control (RTC)** | $< 15\text{ min}$ ($99.99\%$ SLA) | Immediate via secondary bucket / Multi-Region Access Point | Requires S3 Versioning enabled on both source and destination buckets |
| **AWS Elastic Disaster Recovery (DRS)** | Seconds (Continuous block-level replication) | $10\text{--}20\text{ min}$ (Automated staging launch) | Replaces legacy CloudEndure DR; stages into low-cost replication instances |
| **AWS Direct Connect + BFD** | N/A (Network transport) | $< 1\text{ s}$ (3 missed $300\text{ ms}$ heartbeats) | Standard BGP hold-down timer is $90\text{ s}$ without Bidirectional Forwarding Detection (BFD) |

### Declarative Guardrail Comparison: SCP vs. RCP vs. IAM Permission Boundary

A frequent SAP-C03 distractor confuses organizational policies. Remember the exact target of each guardrail:

```text
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ AWS Organization Root / Organizational Unit (OU)                           │
│                                                                            │
│  1. Service Control Policy (SCP):                                          │
│     "What actions can PRINCIPALS inside my Organization invoke?"           │
│                                                                            │
│  2. Resource Control Policy (RCP):                                         │
│     "Who (including EXTERNAL accounts) can access RESOURCES in my Org?"    │
│                                                                            │
│  3. IAM Permissions Boundary:                                              │
│     "What is the maximum privilege an individual IAM Role/User can reach?" │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```
