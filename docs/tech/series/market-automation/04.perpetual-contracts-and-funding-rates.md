## Before you start

A perpetual contract (a "perp") is a bet on an asset's price that never expires and can be taken with borrowed money. You post a deposit, called margin, and control a position several times larger. If you hold a long position and the price falls far enough, the exchange closes your position by force to protect itself. That is liquidation.

Since a perp has no expiry date to pull its price toward the real price, exchanges use a periodic payment between longs and shorts, the funding rate. When the perp trades above spot, longs pay shorts, which makes holding the perp costly and nudges the price back down.

Tiny example: with 10x leverage, a 10 percent price move against you roughly wipes out your deposit.

Read [chapter 2](/tech/series/market-automation/02.order-types-and-financial-precision/) first for orders and the precision rules; chapter 3 covers the price feeds that this chapter's prices come from.

Terms used in this chapter:

- **spot**: buying the asset itself for immediate delivery.
- **perpetual swap (perp)**: a derivative that tracks an asset's price with no expiry date.
- **leverage**: controlling a position larger than your deposit; 10x means ten times the deposit.
- **margin**: the deposit that backs a leveraged position.
- **funding rate**: a periodic payment between long and short holders that keeps the perp price near the spot price.
- **mark price**: a smoothed fair-value price the exchange uses to decide on liquidations, instead of the last traded price.
- **index price**: an average of spot prices across several exchanges.
- **liquidation**: the exchange forcibly closing a position whose margin is too low.

## Perpetual Contracts, Funding Rates & Liquidation Mechanics

In traditional financial markets, futures contracts always have an expiration date (such as quarterly contracts expiring in March, June, September, or December). At expiration, the futures price is forced to converge with the underlying spot price through physical delivery or cash settlement.

In crypto markets, **Perpetual Contracts (Perps)** account for the large majority of derivatives trading volume. Perps have **no expiration date**: a trader can hold a leveraged long or short position open indefinitely.

This raises a fundamental economic question: **if a perpetual contract never expires, what prevents its price from decoupling and drifting wildly away from the actual spot price?**

This chapter breaks down the financial and risk mechanics governing perpetual contracts: how periodic funding rates enforce spot-perp price convergence, why exchanges use **Mark Price** rather than Last Price to prevent "scam wick" liquidations, and how the **Liquidation Waterfall** preserves exchange solvency.

### 1. Spot vs. Futures vs. Perpetual Swaps

To understand perps, compare the three contract structures:

| Attribute | Spot (现货) | Traditional Futures (传统交割期货) | Perpetual Swap (永续合约) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Asset Ownership** | Immediate physical possession | Obligation to settle at future date | Synthetic contract; no asset delivery |
| **Expiration Date** | Never | Fixed date (e.g. `BTC-USD-241227`) | **None (Perpetual)** |
| **Price Anchor** | The asset itself | Converges to spot on expiry date | **Periodic Funding Rate** |
| **Leverage** | $1\times$ (unless borrowing via margin) | $2\times$ to $20\times$ | $1\times$ to $100\times$ |

### 2. The Funding Rate Convergence Mechanism

To keep the perpetual contract trading close to the spot index price, exchanges run a periodic peer-to-peer cash transfer between traders called the **Funding Rate**:

```text
If Perp Price > Spot Price (Bullish Premium):
  Funding Rate is POSITIVE (+)
  -> Longs PAY Shorts!
  -> Holding longs becomes costly; holding shorts is rewarded.
  -> Traders sell perps / close longs, driving the perp price back down to Spot!

If Perp Price < Spot Price (Bearish Discount):
  Funding Rate is NEGATIVE (-)
  -> Shorts PAY Longs!
  -> Traders buy perps / close shorts, driving the perp price back up to Spot!
```

#### The Settlement Interval

On Binance and OKX, funding rate payments settle **every 8 hours by default** (at `00:00`, `08:00`, and `16:00` UTC; both venues move some contracts to shorter intervals such as 4h or 1h when rates hit their caps):

$$
\text{Funding Fee} = \text{Position Size} \times \text{Mark Price} \times \text{Funding Rate}
$$

The exchange **never takes a cut** of this payment; it is purely a zero-sum transfer directly between long and short market participants.

#### The Standard Funding Rate Formula

Binance and OKX calculate the funding rate from two components:

1. **Interest Rate ($I$)**: A fixed baseline interest differential (typically $0.01\%$ per 8 hours, or $0.03\%$ per day).
2. **Premium Index ($P$)**: The percentage deviation between the perp book and the spot index:

$$
P = \frac{\max(0, \text{Impact Bid} - \text{Index Price}) - \max(0, \text{Index Price} - \text{Impact Ask})}{\text{Index Price}}
$$

The final 8-hour rate clamps the premium index within safe bounds:

$$
F = P + \text{clamp}(I - P, -0.05\%, +0.05\%)
$$

Subject to absolute exchange risk bounds (typically clamped to $\pm 0.75\%$ or $\pm 2.0\%$).

### 3. The Three Prices: Index, Last, and Mark Price

A fatal mistake in amateur trading bots is calculating unrealized profit or liquidation against the **Last Trade Price**:

```text
          [ The Danger of Last Price Liquidation ]
  Exchange Order Book:
    Spot Index: $95,000
    Best Bid:   $94,990
    Best Ask:   $95,010
        |
        +---> A rogue trader dumps 100 BTC with an aggressive market order!
        |     The order sweeps thin bids down to $80,000 for 50 milliseconds!
        v
  Last Price momentarily prints: $80,000 ("Scam Wick" / 假针)
```

If the exchange evaluated margin against the Last Price, that 50ms spike would instantly trigger a cascade of margin calls and wipe out all 5x and 10x long accounts across the platform!

#### The Mark Price Defense

To prevent market manipulation, tier-1 exchanges decouple execution from liquidation using **Three Distinct Prices**:

1. **Spot Index Price**: A volume-weighted median of spot prices aggregated across major external exchanges (e.g. Coinbase, Kraken, Bitstamp, OKX, Binance). No single trader can move this price without moving the entire global market.
2. **Last Trade Price**: The price at which the most recent trade occurred on this local matching engine. Used strictly for fill reporting.
3. **Mark Price (Fair Price)**: Used for **unrealized PnL and liquidation monitoring**:

$$
\text{Mark Price} = \text{Spot Index Price} \times \left(1 + \text{Basis Moving Average}\right)
$$

Because Mark Price filters out transient order book spikes, a flash dump in the local order book is far less likely to liquidate your position unless the global spot market also moves. (The formula above is a simplification; Binance, for instance, takes a median of several price estimates.)

### 5. Margin Accounts & The Liquidation Waterfall

When trading with leverage, your deposited collateral is called **Margin**.

- **Initial Margin Ratio (IMR)**: The minimum collateral required to open a position. At $10\times$ leverage, $\text{IMR} = 10\%$.
- **Maintenance Margin Ratio (MMR)**: The absolute minimum collateral required to keep the position open. Typically half of IMR (e.g. $5\%$ or $2.5\%$).

$$
\text{Margin Ratio} = \frac{\text{Maintenance Margin}}{\text{Account Equity}}
$$

#### The Liquidation Waterfall

When an adverse market move causes your Margin Ratio to hit $100\%$ ($\text{Account Equity} \le \text{Maintenance Margin}$), the exchange's automated risk engine takes over the account through a multi-stage **Liquidation Waterfall**:

1. **Cancel Open Orders**: All resting orders for that symbol are canceled immediately to free up reserved margin.
2. **Margin Call / Tier Reduction**: The exchange attempts to execute a partial liquidation to step down to a lower position bracket with a lower MMR.
3. **Liquidation Order (Takeover)**: If equity remains below MMR, the exchange takes over the position at the **Bankruptcy Price** (the price where account equity equals exactly zero).
4. **The Insurance Fund**:
   - If the liquidation order executes at a price *better* than the bankruptcy price, the leftover profit is deposited into the exchange's **Insurance Fund**.
   - If the market is crashing so fast that the order fills *worse* than the bankruptcy price, the Insurance Fund pays the deficit.
5. **Auto-Deleveraging (ADL)**: If the Insurance Fund is completely depleted during an extreme black swan event, the engine triggers ADL: it automatically closes the positions of opposing, highly profitable, highly leveraged traders to balance the ledger.

### 6. Verification Lab: Implementing a Perpetual Funding & Liquidation Engine

The following Python script implements a simplified derivatives risk engine:

- Aggregates multi-exchange spot feeds into a median Spot Index.
- Calculates fair Mark Price.
- Evaluates 8-hour funding rates with clamping and executes zero-sum peer transfers.
- Tracks a leveraged margin position: calculates unrealized PnL, Maintenance Margin Ratio, exact Liquidation Price, and evaluates liquidation triggers.

```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""
Lab: Perpetual Contract Funding Rate & Liquidation Waterfall Simulator
Verifies Spot Index median, clamped funding rate settlement, and margin liquidation.
"""

from dataclasses import dataclass
from typing import List, Tuple
import statistics

@dataclass
class Position:
    symbol: str
    side: str        # "LONG" or "SHORT"
    qty: float       # In base currency (e.g. BTC)
    entry_price: float
    leverage: float
    collateral: float  # Margin deposited (USDT)

    def calculate_unrealized_pnl(self, mark_price: float) -> float:
        if self.side == "LONG":
            return (mark_price - self.entry_price) * self.qty
        else:
            return (self.entry_price - mark_price) * self.qty

    def calculate_equity(self, mark_price: float) -> float:
        return self.collateral + self.calculate_unrealized_pnl(mark_price)

    def calculate_liquidation_price(self, mmr: float) -> float:
        """
        Mark price L at which Equity == Qty * L * MMR (the same test check_liquidation applies).
        LONG:  collateral + (L - entry) * qty = qty * L * mmr
        SHORT: collateral + (entry - L) * qty = qty * L * mmr
        """
        if self.side == "LONG":
            return (self.entry_price * self.qty - self.collateral) / (self.qty * (1 - mmr))
        else:
            return (self.entry_price * self.qty + self.collateral) / (self.qty * (1 + mmr))

class PerpetualRiskEngine:
    def __init__(self, mmr: float = 0.05):  # 5% Maintenance Margin Ratio
        self.mmr = mmr

    def calculate_spot_index(self, exchange_spot_prices: List[float]) -> float:
        """Robust median price across multiple external exchanges."""
        return statistics.median(exchange_spot_prices)

    def calculate_funding_rate(self, perp_price: float, spot_index: float) -> float:
        """
        Simplified Binance-style funding rate: F = P + clamp(I - P, -0.05%, +0.05%),
        then capped to +/-0.75%. P is approximated as (Perp - Spot) / Spot.
        """
        premium = (perp_price - spot_index) / spot_index
        interest_rate = 0.0001  # 0.01% baseline interest per 8 hours
        rate = premium + max(-0.0005, min(0.0005, interest_rate - premium))
        return max(-0.0075, min(0.0075, rate))

    def settle_funding(self, long_pos: Position, short_pos: Position, mark_price: float, rate: float):
        """Executes zero-sum peer-to-peer funding settlement."""
        notional = long_pos.qty * mark_price
        funding_fee = notional * rate

        # Positive rate: Long pays Short
        long_pos.collateral -= funding_fee
        short_pos.collateral += funding_fee
        return funding_fee

    def check_liquidation(self, pos: Position, mark_price: float) -> Tuple[bool, float]:
        """
        Returns (is_liquidated, margin_ratio).
        Liquidates when Equity <= Position Notional * MMR.
        """
        equity = pos.calculate_equity(mark_price)
        maintenance_margin = pos.qty * mark_price * self.mmr
        margin_ratio = maintenance_margin / equity if equity > 0 else 999.0
        is_liquidated = equity <= maintenance_margin
        return is_liquidated, margin_ratio

def test_perpetual_engine():
    engine = PerpetualRiskEngine(mmr=0.05)  # 5% MMR (up to 20x leverage)

    # 1. Multi-Exchange Spot Index Median Calculation
    # Feeds from Coinbase ($95,010), Kraken ($95,000), OKX ($94,990), Binance ($95,000)
    spot_feeds = [95010.0, 95000.0, 94990.0, 95000.0]
    spot_index = engine.calculate_spot_index(spot_feeds)
    print("=== 1. Spot Index Median Calculation ===")
    print(f"  Spot Feeds: {spot_feeds} -> Median Index: ${spot_index:.2f}")
    assert spot_index == 95000.0

    # 2. Funding Rate Clamping Verification
    # Perp is trading at a premium: $95,500 vs Spot $95,000 (+0.526% premium)
    perp_price = 95500.0
    funding_rate = engine.calculate_funding_rate(perp_price, spot_index)
    print("\n=== 2. Funding Rate Calculation ===")
    print(f"  Perp=${perp_price}, Spot=${spot_index} -> Funding Rate = {funding_rate * 100:.4f}%")
    assert funding_rate > 0, "Positive premium must yield positive funding rate"

    # Extreme premium test: $100,000 perp vs $95,000 spot (+5.26% premium)
    extreme_rate = engine.calculate_funding_rate(100000.0, spot_index)
    # Must be capped at 0.75%
    assert extreme_rate == 0.0075, f"Funding rate exceeded clamp: {extreme_rate}"
    print(f"  Extreme Premium ($100k): Clamped to {extreme_rate * 100:.4f}%")

    # 3. Position Sizing & Liquidation Price
    print("\n=== 3. Position Margin & Liquidation Calculation ===")
    # Trader opens 10x Long: 1.0 BTC @ $95,000 with $9,500 collateral (10x leverage)
    long_pos = Position(
        symbol="BTCUSDT-PERP",
        side="LONG",
        qty=1.0,
        entry_price=95000.0,
        leverage=10.0,
        collateral=9500.0
    )
    liq_price = long_pos.calculate_liquidation_price(mmr=0.05)
    print(f"  Entry: $95,000, Collateral: $9,500, MMR: 5%")
    print(f"  Calculated Liquidation Price: ${liq_price:.2f}")
    # At Liq: 9500 + (Liq - 95000) == Liq * 0.05  ->  Liq = 85,500 / 0.95 = $90,000
    assert abs(liq_price - 90000.0) < 1e-6

    # 4. Peer-to-Peer Funding Settlement
    short_pos = Position("BTCUSDT-PERP", "SHORT", 1.0, 95000.0, 10.0, 9500.0)
    fee_paid = engine.settle_funding(long_pos, short_pos, mark_price=95000.0, rate=funding_rate)
    print("\n=== 4. 8-Hour Funding Settlement ===")
    print(f"  Long collateral after paying fee:  ${long_pos.collateral:.2f}")
    print(f"  Short collateral after receipt:    ${short_pos.collateral:.2f}")
    assert long_pos.collateral == 9500.0 - fee_paid
    assert short_pos.collateral == 9500.0 + fee_paid

    # 5. Liquidation Trigger Test (fresh position, so funding above does not shift the numbers)
    print("\n=== 5. Mark Price Drop & Liquidation Waterfall ===")
    long_pos = Position("BTCUSDT-PERP", "LONG", 1.0, 95000.0, 10.0, 9500.0)
    # Market drops to $92,000 (Safe, above liq price)
    is_liq, ratio = engine.check_liquidation(long_pos, mark_price=92000.0)
    assert is_liq is False
    print(f"  Mark Price $92,000: Margin Ratio = {ratio * 100:.1f}% (SAFE)")

    # Market crashes to $89,900 (Crossed below liquidation price $90,000!)
    is_liq_crash, ratio_crash = engine.check_liquidation(long_pos, mark_price=89900.0)
    assert is_liq_crash is True
    print(f"  Mark Price $89,900: Margin Ratio = {ratio_crash * 100:.1f}% -> LIQUIDATION TRIGGERED!")

    print("\nAll Perpetual Contract, Funding Rate & Liquidation tests PASSED!")

if __name__ == "__main__":
    test_perpetual_engine()
```

### 7. Summary & Key Takeaways

1. **The 8-Hour Anchor**: Perpetual contracts maintain price parity with the spot market purely through peer-to-peer funding transfers without ever expiring.
2. **Mark Price Defends Solvency**: Liquidations are evaluated against a mark price built from the external Spot Index plus a basis term, which largely insulates positions from local order book flash-crashes.
3. **The Margin Invariant**: Never allow an account's equity to fall below Maintenance Margin. The liquidation engine intervenes early to prevent deficit balances and protect exchange solvency.
