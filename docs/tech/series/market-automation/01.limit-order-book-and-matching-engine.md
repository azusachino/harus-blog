## Before you start

This series is for programmers who want to build automated trading software and have never worked inside an exchange. The chapters build on each other, from the order book up to risk controls and simulation.

An exchange does not hold one price. It holds two sorted lists of standing orders: buyers who each say "I will pay up to this much" and sellers who each say "I will sell for at least this much". The best buy price (the bid) and the best sell price (the ask) are the top of each list. A trade happens when a new order crosses the gap between them.

Tiny example: bids of 100 for 5 units and 99 for 10 units, asks of 101 for 3 units. A new buy order at 101 for 2 units matches immediately against the ask, leaving 1 unit there. This chapter builds that list, the rule for who gets matched first, and the data structures that make it fast.

You need only basic programming; no finance background is assumed beyond the terms below.

Terms used in this chapter:

- **order book**: the exchange's list of all standing buy and sell orders, grouped by price.
- **limit order**: an order to buy or sell at a given price or better, which waits in the book if it cannot be filled at once.
- **bid / ask**: the highest price a buyer currently offers, and the lowest price a seller currently asks.
- **spread**: the gap between the best ask and the best bid; crossing it is a cost of trading.
- **matching engine**: the exchange program that pairs incoming orders with resting ones.
- **price-time priority**: the rule that better prices fill first, and among equal prices the older order fills first.
- **market depth**: how much quantity is waiting at each price level.

## The Limit Order Book & Matching Engine

In popular media, the price of an asset is often spoken of as a single number: *"Bitcoin is trading at \$95,000"* or *"Apple is \$220"*.

In an electronic exchange, **there is no single price**. An exchange does not buy or sell assets; it operates a centralized ledger of intentions called the **Limit Order Book (LOB)**. The "current price" is simply the agreed boundary where buyers and sellers are currently negotiating.

This first chapter examines how modern electronic matching engines operate from first principles: how resting orders form price ladders, how the bid-ask spread dictates transaction costs, and how the **Price-Time Priority (FIFO)** matching algorithm deterministically executes trades.

### 1. Demystifying the Order Book

An order book maintains two continuous queues of passive orders:

- **Bids (Buyers)**: Traders offering to purchase an asset at or below their stated limit price.
- **Asks / Offers (Sellers)**: Traders offering to sell an asset at or above their stated limit price.

```text
          [ ASKS / SELLERS ]
   Price ($)       Quantity (BTC)    Total Depth ($)
   95,003.00           1.50             142,504.50
   95,002.00           0.80              76,001.60
   95,001.00           0.50              47,500.50  <-- Best Ask (Lowest Seller)
--------------------------------------------------  <-- Spread: $2.00 (Cost of Immediacy)
   94,999.00           1.20             113,998.80  <-- Best Bid (Highest Buyer)
   94,998.00           2.00             189,996.00
   94,995.00           5.00             474,975.00
          [ BIDS / BUYERS ]
```

#### The Bid-Ask Spread

Notice the gap between the highest buyer (\$94,999.00) and the lowest seller (\$95,001.00):

$$
\text{Spread} = \text{Best Ask} - \text{Best Bid} = \$95,001.00 - \$94,999.00 = \$2.00
$$

The bid-ask spread represents the **friction of liquidity**:

- If you demand immediate execution, you cannot buy at \$94,999.00; you must pay the seller's asking price of \$95,001.00.
- If you immediately sell that asset back, you receive only \$94,999.00 from the highest buyer.
- You instantly forfeit \$2.00 per unit. That cost goes to **Market Makers**, who provide liquidity by quoting both sides of the book and absorbing inventory risk.

### 2. Market Depth: Level 1, Level 2, and Level 3

Exchanges broadcast market data at different tiers of granularity:

| Depth Level | Name | Information Provided | Real-World Venue Equivalent |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Level 1 (L1)** | **BBO (Best Bid / Offer)** | Highest bid price/quantity and lowest ask price/quantity only. | Binance `bookTicker`, OKX `tickers` |
| **Level 2 (L2)** | **Aggregated Depth** | Cumulative volume available at each price level (top 20, 50, or full book). | Binance `depthUpdate`, OKX `books50-l2-tbt` |
| **Level 3 (L3)** | **Order-by-Order** | Every individual resting order with unique IDs, sequence numbers, and timestamps. | NASDAQ ITCH, Coinbase Market Data Feed |

#### Real-World Sample: Binance Level 2 Depth Payload

When querying Binance REST API (`GET /api/v3/depth?symbol=BTCUSDT&limit=5`):

```json
{
  "lastUpdateId": 1027021453,
  "bids": [
    [ "94999.00", "1.20000000" ],
    [ "94998.00", "2.00000000" ]
  ],
  "asks": [
    [ "95001.00", "0.50000000" ],
    [ "95002.00", "0.80000000" ]
  ]
}
```

Each price level aggregates all resting limit orders sitting at that exact price into a single total quantity.

### 3. The Price-Time Priority (FIFO) Matching Algorithm

How does an exchange decide which orders execute when a new order arrives? Modern electronic markets almost universally use **Price-Time Priority (FIFO)**:

1. **Price Priority**: The best price always fills first.
   - For buyers: a buyer willing to pay \$95,000 fills before a buyer willing to pay \$94,990.
   - For sellers: a seller willing to accept \$95,000 fills before a seller demanding \$95,010.
2. **Time Priority (FIFO)**: If multiple traders submit orders at the **exact same price**, the order that arrived at the exchange first fills first.

```text
Incoming Market Order: BUY 2.0 BTC

Step 1: Check Best Ask ($95,001.00, Alice: 0.5 BTC)
        -> Filled 0.5 BTC @ $95,001.00 (Alice completely filled & removed)
        -> Remaining needed: 1.5 BTC

Step 2: Check Next Ask ($95,002.00, Bob: 0.8 BTC, Charlie: 0.7 BTC)
        -> Bob arrived at 10:00:01 (Time priority!)
        -> Filled 0.8 BTC @ $95,002.00 (Bob completely filled & removed)
        -> Remaining needed: 0.7 BTC

Step 3: Charlie arrived at 10:00:02
        -> Filled 0.7 BTC @ $95,002.00 (Charlie partially filled)
        -> Incoming order fully executed across 3 fills!
```

### 4. High-Performance Order Book Data Structures

A naive array of orders requires an $\mathcal{O}(N)$ scan to find matching prices, and an $\mathcal{O}(N)$ shift to delete filled orders. At tens of thousands of messages per second, this introduces catastrophic latency spikes.

High-frequency matching engines (such as in Rust or C++) structure the order book as a **Composite Hierarchy**:

```text
[ LimitOrderBook ]
  ├── bids: SortedMap<Price, PriceLevel>  (Sorted descending)
  └── asks: SortedMap<Price, PriceLevel>  (Sorted ascending)

      PriceLevel ($95,000.00):
        total_volume: 3.5 BTC
        order_count: 2
        orders: DoublyLinkedList<Order>
          ├── Order A (2.0 BTC, ts: 1000)  <-- Head (Oldest, fills first)
          └── Order B (1.5 BTC, ts: 1001)  <-- Tail (Newest)
```

1. **Price Ladder (`SortedMap` / Red-Black Tree / B-Tree)**: Allows locating the Best Bid or Best Ask in $\mathcal{O}(1)$ time (with a cached best-price pointer; $\mathcal{O}(\log P)$ otherwise), and inserting a new price level in $\mathcal{O}(\log P)$ time (where $P$ is the number of distinct price levels, typically small).
2. **FIFO Queue (`DoublyLinkedList`)**: Appending a new order to the back of a price level is $\mathcal{O}(1)$. Popping the head order during execution is $\mathcal{O}(1)$. Canceling an order anywhere in the queue is $\mathcal{O}(1)$ when a hash map from order ID to list node is kept alongside.

### 5. Verification Lab: Building a Deterministic Matching Engine

The following Python script implements a self-contained matching engine:

- Full LOB price ladder (descending bids, ascending asks).
- FIFO queue per price level preserving time priority.
- Aggressive crossing execution producing atomic `Trade` fill records.
- Level 2 depth snapshot extractor matching Binance/OKX data formats.

```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""
Lab: Limit Order Book & Price-Time Priority Matching Engine
Verifies price priority, FIFO queue execution, and Level 2 depth aggregation.
"""

from collections import deque
from dataclasses import dataclass
from typing import Dict, List, Optional, Tuple

@dataclass
class Order:
    order_id: str
    side: str       # "BUY" or "SELL"
    price: float
    qty: float
    timestamp: int

@dataclass
class Trade:
    maker_order_id: str
    taker_order_id: str
    price: float
    qty: float

class PriceLevel:
    def __init__(self, price: float):
        self.price = price
        self.total_qty = 0.0
        self.orders: deque[Order] = deque()

    def add(self, order: Order):
        self.orders.append(order)
        self.total_qty += order.qty

    def is_empty(self) -> bool:
        return len(self.orders) == 0

class MatchingEngine:
    def __init__(self):
        # Bids sorted high to low (descending)
        self.bids: Dict[float, PriceLevel] = {}
        # Asks sorted low to high (ascending)
        self.asks: Dict[float, PriceLevel] = {}

    def get_best_bid(self) -> Optional[float]:
        return max(self.bids.keys()) if self.bids else None

    def get_best_ask(self) -> Optional[float]:
        return min(self.asks.keys()) if self.asks else None

    def submit_order(self, order: Order) -> Tuple[List[Trade], Optional[Order]]:
        trades: List[Trade] = []

        if order.side == "BUY":
            # Match against lowest asks
            while order.qty > 0 and self.asks:
                best_ask_price = self.get_best_ask()
                if best_ask_price is None or order.price < best_ask_price:
                    break  # Cannot cross

                level = self.asks[best_ask_price]
                while order.qty > 0 and level.orders:
                    maker = level.orders[0]
                    fill_qty = min(order.qty, maker.qty)

                    trades.append(Trade(
                        maker_order_id=maker.order_id,
                        taker_order_id=order.order_id,
                        price=maker.price,
                        qty=fill_qty
                    ))

                    order.qty -= fill_qty
                    maker.qty -= fill_qty
                    level.total_qty -= fill_qty

                    if maker.qty == 0:
                        level.orders.popleft()

                if level.is_empty():
                    del self.asks[best_ask_price]

            # Rest remaining quantity on the bid ladder
            if order.qty > 0:
                if order.price not in self.bids:
                    self.bids[order.price] = PriceLevel(order.price)
                self.bids[order.price].add(order)
                return trades, order

        elif order.side == "SELL":
            # Match against highest bids
            while order.qty > 0 and self.bids:
                best_bid_price = self.get_best_bid()
                if best_bid_price is None or order.price > best_bid_price:
                    break  # Cannot cross

                level = self.bids[best_bid_price]
                while order.qty > 0 and level.orders:
                    maker = level.orders[0]
                    fill_qty = min(order.qty, maker.qty)

                    trades.append(Trade(
                        maker_order_id=maker.order_id,
                        taker_order_id=order.order_id,
                        price=maker.price,
                        qty=fill_qty
                    ))

                    order.qty -= fill_qty
                    maker.qty -= fill_qty
                    level.total_qty -= fill_qty

                    if maker.qty == 0:
                        level.orders.popleft()

                if level.is_empty():
                    del self.bids[best_bid_price]

            # Rest remaining quantity on the ask ladder
            if order.qty > 0:
                if order.price not in self.asks:
                    self.asks[order.price] = PriceLevel(order.price)
                self.asks[order.price].add(order)
                return trades, order

        return trades, None

    def get_l2_depth(self, depth: int = 5) -> Dict[str, List[Tuple[float, float]]]:
        """Returns Binance-compatible Level 2 depth snapshot."""
        sorted_bids = sorted(self.bids.keys(), reverse=True)[:depth]
        sorted_asks = sorted(self.asks.keys())[:depth]
        return {
            "bids": [(p, self.bids[p].total_qty) for p in sorted_bids],
            "asks": [(p, self.asks[p].total_qty) for p in sorted_asks]
        }

def test_matching_engine():
    engine = MatchingEngine()

    # 1. Place passive resting asks
    # Alice quotes 1.0 BTC @ $95,000 at timestamp 100
    engine.submit_order(Order("order-alice", "SELL", 95000.0, 1.0, timestamp=100))
    # Bob quotes 1.0 BTC @ $95,000 at timestamp 101 (same price, later time!)
    engine.submit_order(Order("order-bob", "SELL", 95000.0, 1.0, timestamp=101))
    # Charlie quotes 2.0 BTC @ $95,010 at timestamp 102 (higher price)
    engine.submit_order(Order("order-charlie", "SELL", 95010.0, 2.0, timestamp=102))

    # 2. Verify Level 2 depth snapshot
    depth = engine.get_l2_depth()
    print("=== 1. Initial Level 2 Asks Depth ===")
    print(f"  Asks: {depth['asks']}")
    assert depth['asks'][0] == (95000.0, 2.0)  # Alice + Bob = 2.0 BTC
    assert depth['asks'][1] == (95010.0, 2.0)

    # 3. Aggressive incoming market-like Buy order
    # David wants to buy 1.5 BTC up to $95,005
    print("\n=== 2. Price-Time Priority Execution ===")
    trades, resting = engine.submit_order(Order("order-david", "BUY", 95005.0, 1.5, timestamp=200))

    # Invariants:
    # 1. Alice must fill first (1.0 BTC) due to Time Priority
    # 2. Bob must fill second (0.5 BTC partial)
    # 3. Charlie must NOT fill because Charlie's ask ($95,010) > David's limit ($95,005)
    assert len(trades) == 2
    assert trades[0].maker_order_id == "order-alice" and trades[0].qty == 1.0
    assert trades[1].maker_order_id == "order-bob" and trades[1].qty == 0.5
    print(f"  Trade 1: {trades[0].qty} BTC filled against {trades[0].maker_order_id} @ {trades[0].price}")
    print(f"  Trade 2: {trades[1].qty} BTC filled against {trades[1].maker_order_id} @ {trades[1].price}")

    # 4. Check remaining book depth
    depth_after = engine.get_l2_depth()
    print("\n=== 3. Post-Trade Depth State ===")
    print(f"  Asks: {depth_after['asks']}")
    assert depth_after['asks'][0] == (95000.0, 0.5)  # Bob has 0.5 BTC remaining
    assert depth_after['asks'][1] == (95010.0, 2.0)  # Charlie untouched

    print("\nAll Limit Order Book & Matching Engine tests PASSED!")

if __name__ == "__main__":
    test_matching_engine()
```

### 6. Summary & Key Takeaways

1. **The Order Book is the Reality**: Prices are not continuous functions; they are discrete queues of resting limit orders separated by the bid-ask spread.
2. **Cost of Immediacy**: Crossing the spread guarantees execution but costs the spread plus taker fees; passive quoting earns maker rebates but introduces queue wait times and adverse selection risk.
3. **Price-Time Priority Invariance**: Bids match the lowest asks first. Equal prices execute strictly in chronological order of arrival (FIFO).
4. **Data Structure Discipline**: Combining sorted price ladders with doubly-linked order lists guarantees deterministic $\mathcal{O}(1)$ execution and cancellation.
