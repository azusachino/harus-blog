## Before you start

This chapter has two problems. The first is supporting several exchanges and brokers from one program. It is tempting to write one interface that hides all the differences, but a crypto spot exchange, a perpetual futures market and a Tokyo-listed stock follow different rules for lot sizes, trading hours and margin. The chapter argues for an interface in which each venue states honestly what it can and cannot do.

The second problem is testing without real money. Paper trading is a simulated account, and a naive one flatters you: it fills your order instantly at the price you see, ignoring that you would have waited in line behind other orders. The chapter shows a simulator that models queue position so its results are closer to what would really happen.

Read [chapter 1](/tech/series/market-automation/01.limit-order-book-and-matching-engine/) first for the order book and queues, and [chapter 5](/tech/series/market-automation/05.pre-trade-risk-controls-and-kill-switches/) for the checks that wrap every order.

Terms used in this chapter:

- **venue**: any exchange or broker where orders can be placed.
- **abstraction**: a common interface that lets the rest of the program ignore venue-specific details.
- **capability contract**: a declaration of which features a venue supports, so unsupported requests fail clearly.
- **paper trading**: running a strategy against simulated orders and balances instead of real ones.
- **queue position**: how many orders are ahead of yours at the same price; it determines when you get filled.
- **board lot**: the fixed number of shares a stock must be traded in, such as 100 on Japanese exchanges.
- **reconciliation**: comparing your records of orders, fills and balances with the venue's to find differences.
- **ledger**: the record of every balance change, which must add up with what the venue reports.

## Venue Abstraction & Realistic Paper Simulation

When developers begin building market automation tools, the most common trap is chasing the fantasy of a **"Universal Exchange API"**: a single monolithic abstraction that purports to treat Binance, OKX, Interactive Brokers, and Japanese equities (TSE via moomoo OpenD) as identical black boxes.

In reality, **exchanges have fundamentally divergent execution models**:

- Crypto spot exchanges use WebSocket feeds and balance-checked spot wallets.
- Crypto derivatives use isolated/cross margin accounts, continuous funding rates, and multi-tier liquidation engines.
- Japanese brokerages (such as moomoo or SBI) enforce trading sessions, daily price limit bands (ストップ高/ストップ安), board lot sizes (100 shares), and proprietary binary/protobuf gateways.

Attempting to hide these differences behind generic stubs leads to subtle production bugs.

This final chapter explores the **provider-neutral architecture** used in my `dandelion` workspace: how to model truthful capability contracts, how to construct a **realistic paper trading simulation** that accounts for queue position and network latency, and how to verify post-trade ledger invariance.

### 1. The Dandelion Pattern: Truthful Capability Contracts

Rather than forcing every exchange into a single massive interface, high-reliability trading engines decompose functionality into **focused, composable behaviors**:

```rust
// Focused, truthful capability traits
pub trait InstrumentCatalog {
    async fn fetch_instruments(&self) -> Result<Vec<Instrument>>;
}

pub trait MarketData {
    async fn stream_depth(&self, symbol: &str) -> Result<Receiver<DepthUpdate>>;
}

pub trait SpotOrders {
    async fn submit_order(&self, order: &OrderRequest) -> Result<ExecutionReport>;
    async fn cancel_order(&self, cloid: &str) -> Result<()>;
}
```

#### The Invariant of Truthful Capabilities

In a production workspace:

- **Binance & OKX Adapters**: Might implement `InstrumentCatalog` and `MarketData` for public streaming, but leave `SpotOrders` unimplemented if authenticated API keys are not configured.
- **Moomoo Adapter**: Might implement a TCP readiness probe to the local OpenD gateway, but declare trading capabilities as *planned* until official protobuf sessions are verified.
- **Paper Provider**: Implements all traits as a deterministic local test double.

Unsupported behavior is **completely absent from the type contract**, rather than hidden behind a runtime boolean or a compiling stub that returns an unexpected runtime error.

### 2. Why Naive Paper Trading Lies to You

Many quantitative backtests and paper trading bots show spectacular theoretical profits, only to bleed capital when deployed to live exchanges.

Why? Because naive simulators rely on **unrealistic execution assumptions**:

```text
The Naive Sim Trap:
"Market price touched my limit order at $95,000 for one millisecond, so I was 100% filled with zero slippage!"
```

In real electronic markets, this assumption is completely false:

1. **Queue Priority**: If 10.0 BTC was resting at \$95,000 *before* you submitted your limit order, your order sits at the back of the line. If incoming market sells only consume 2.0 BTC before the price bounces back up to \$95,050, **your order filled exactly 0.0 BTC**.
2. **Network & Matching Latency**: It takes 5 to 50 milliseconds for your packet to cross the internet and enter the matching engine. In volatile markets, the quote you saw on your screen may already be gone before your packet arrives.
3. **Taker Market Impact**: A large market order does not fill at the Best Ask; it sweeps the book, paying slippage on every level it consumes.

### 3. High-Fidelity Paper Simulation Architecture

A production paper trading simulator must model real-world market friction:

```text
[ New Limit Order: BUY 1.0 BTC @ $95,000 ]
                    |
                    v
    1. Network Latency Delay (e.g. 15ms wire transit)
                    |
                    v
    2. Inspect Current Book Depth at $95,000:
       - Existing resting volume: 4.5 BTC ahead of us in queue!
       - Store Queue Ahead = 4.5 BTC
                    |
                    v
    3. Listen to Continuous Public Trade Stream:
       - Trade prints: 1.0 BTC @ $95,000 -> Queue Ahead = 3.5 BTC
       - Trade prints: 2.5 BTC @ $95,000 -> Queue Ahead = 1.0 BTC
       - Trade prints: 1.5 BTC @ $95,000 -> Queue Ahead depleted!
         -> We fill 0.5 BTC! (Partial fill)
```

By tracking **Queue Volume Ahead**, the simulator ensures that paper orders only fill when real market trading volume has genuinely depleted all preceding resting orders.

### 4. Post-Trade Reconciliation & Ledger Invariance

After trades execute, the automated system must continuously reconcile its local state against exchange truth:

$$
\text{Expected Cash Balance} = \text{Initial Cash} - \sum (\text{Filled Qty} \times \text{Price}) - \sum \text{Fees Paid}
$$

Any discrepancy between the local ledger and the exchange's reported balance triggers an immediate circuit breaker.

### 5. Verification Lab: Implementing a Queue-Aware Paper Execution Simulator

The following Python script implements a queue-aware paper trading simulator:

- Decomposes venue capabilities using clean interfaces.
- Implements **Queue-Aware Fill Simulation**: when a passive limit order is placed, it records the exact volume resting ahead of it.
- Consumes incoming trade events: paper orders only fill once the preceding queue volume has been fully consumed by real market trades.
- Rejects overly optimistic fills when market trades do not reach the required queue depth.

The model is deliberately simple. It ignores cancellations ahead of you (which would move you up the queue, so it errs on the pessimistic side) and treats every trade print as unrelated to your order's own impact.

```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""
Lab: Queue-Aware Paper Trading Simulator & Venue Abstraction
Verifies queue-ahead depletion, partial fills, and realistic execution boundaries.
"""

from dataclasses import dataclass
from typing import Dict, List, Optional, Tuple

@dataclass
class PaperOrder:
    cloid: str
    symbol: str
    side: str        # "BUY" or "SELL"
    price: float
    qty: float
    filled_qty: float = 0.0
    queue_ahead: float = 0.0  # Volume resting ahead of us at this price level
    status: str = "OPEN"      # "OPEN", "PARTIALLY_FILLED", "FILLED", "CANCELED"

class QueueAwarePaperSimulator:
    """
    High-fidelity paper execution engine.
    Does NOT assume instant fills: requires real trade volume to deplete queue_ahead.
    """
    def __init__(self):
        self.open_orders: Dict[str, PaperOrder] = {}
        self.closed_orders: Dict[str, PaperOrder] = {}

    def submit_limit_order(
        self,
        cloid: str,
        symbol: str,
        side: str,
        price: float,
        qty: float,
        current_book_depth_at_price: float
    ) -> PaperOrder:
        order = PaperOrder(
            cloid=cloid,
            symbol=symbol,
            side=side,
            price=price,
            qty=qty,
            queue_ahead=current_book_depth_at_price
        )
        self.open_orders[cloid] = order
        print(f"  [paper-order] Submitted {cloid} ({side} {qty} @ {price}), Queue Ahead = {current_book_depth_at_price} units")
        return order

    def on_market_trade(self, price: float, trade_qty: float):
        """
        Processes real-world market trade prints to advance queue position and fills.
        """
        for cloid, order in list(self.open_orders.items()):
            # Only trades at or better than our limit price can deplete queue
            is_matching_price = (order.side == "BUY" and price <= order.price) or \
                                (order.side == "SELL" and price >= order.price)

            if not is_matching_price:
                continue

            # A print strictly through our price means we were ahead of that level's queue
            if (order.side == "BUY" and price < order.price) or \
                    (order.side == "SELL" and price > order.price):
                order.queue_ahead = 0.0

            # First, trade volume depletes queue ahead of us
            if order.queue_ahead > 0:
                consumed = min(order.queue_ahead, trade_qty)
                order.queue_ahead -= consumed
                remaining_trade_qty = trade_qty - consumed
            else:
                remaining_trade_qty = trade_qty

            # If queue ahead is depleted, trade volume fills our order!
            if remaining_trade_qty > 0 and order.queue_ahead == 0:
                needed = order.qty - order.filled_qty
                fill = min(needed, remaining_trade_qty)
                order.filled_qty += fill
                print(f"  [paper-fill] Order {cloid} filled +{fill:.2f} (Total filled: {order.filled_qty:.2f}/{order.qty})")

                if order.filled_qty >= order.qty:
                    order.status = "FILLED"
                    self.closed_orders[cloid] = order
                    del self.open_orders[cloid]
                    print(f"  [paper-complete] Order {cloid} COMPLETELY FILLED!")
                else:
                    order.status = "PARTIALLY_FILLED"

def test_paper_simulation():
    sim = QueueAwarePaperSimulator()

    # 1. Place a passive BUY limit order: 1.0 BTC @ $95,000
    # There is currently 2.0 BTC resting ahead of us in the order book at $95,000
    print("=== 1. Placing Limit Order Behind Existing Queue ===")
    order = sim.submit_limit_order(
        cloid="strat_order_01",
        symbol="BTCUSDT",
        side="BUY",
        price=95000.0,
        qty=1.0,
        current_book_depth_at_price=2.0  # 2.0 BTC ahead of us
    )
    assert order.status == "OPEN"
    assert order.filled_qty == 0.0

    # 2. A market trade prints: 1.5 BTC @ $95,000
    # In a naive simulator, the bot would falsely fill!
    # In our queue-aware simulator, it only depletes the queue ahead from 2.0 -> 0.5 BTC.
    print("\n=== 2. Market Trade #1 (1.5 BTC @ $95,000) ===")
    sim.on_market_trade(price=95000.0, trade_qty=1.5)
    assert order.status == "OPEN"
    assert order.filled_qty == 0.0, "Must not fill before queue ahead is depleted!"
    assert order.queue_ahead == 0.5
    print(f"  Result: Order NOT filled yet. Queue ahead reduced to {order.queue_ahead} BTC.")

    # 3. Market Trade #2: 1.0 BTC @ $95,000
    # Consumes the remaining 0.5 BTC queue ahead, then fills 0.5 BTC of our order!
    print("\n=== 3. Market Trade #2 (1.0 BTC @ $95,000) ===")
    sim.on_market_trade(price=95000.0, trade_qty=1.0)
    assert order.status == "PARTIALLY_FILLED"
    assert order.filled_qty == 0.5
    assert order.queue_ahead == 0.0

    # 4. Market Trade #3: 2.0 BTC @ $94,990 (Aggressive drop below our limit)
    # The remaining 0.5 BTC of our order fills completely!
    print("\n=== 4. Market Trade #3 (2.0 BTC @ $94,990) ===")
    sim.on_market_trade(price=94990.0, trade_qty=2.0)
    assert "strat_order_01" not in sim.open_orders
    completed = sim.closed_orders["strat_order_01"]
    assert completed.status == "FILLED"
    assert completed.filled_qty == 1.0

    print("\nAll Queue-Aware Paper Execution Simulation tests PASSED!")

if __name__ == "__main__":
    test_paper_simulation()
```
